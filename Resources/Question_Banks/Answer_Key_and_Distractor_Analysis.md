# Answer Key and Distractor Analysis Guide

This guide explains why common wrong answers fail in Security+ scenarios.

## How to Use This File

1. Attempt question banks first.
2. Check your answer.
3. Compare your reasoning against the distractor patterns below.
4. Record recurring mistakes in the remediation tracker.

## Top Distractor Patterns

| Distractor Pattern                         | Why It Looks Correct             | Why It Is Wrong                                       |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Overly broad control                       | Sounds decisive and strong       | Often violates least privilege or business continuity |
| Future-state project step                  | Appears strategic                | Ignores immediate incident lifecycle needs            |
| Policy-level response for technical prompt | Governance language seems formal | Prompt requires operational/technical action          |
| Metric instead of control                  | Quantifies outcome               | Does not implement prevention/detection itself        |
| Correct concept, wrong sequence            | Uses valid terms                 | Order is unsafe or inefficient for scenario           |

## Domain-Specific Distractor Heuristics

### Domain 1

- Wrong answers often confuse integrity vs confidentiality.
- Hashing is often presented as an encryption substitute.
- Policy/standard/procedure terms are frequently swapped.

### Domain 2

- CVSS-only prioritization is a frequent trap.
- "Patch everything immediately" can be unrealistic and unsafe.
- Threat category labels may be close but context-disconnected.

### Domain 3

- Most secure sounding architecture may break availability requirements.
- Perimeter-only designs are often insufficient in modern scenarios.
- RTO and RPO are commonly reversed.

### Domain 4

- Full eradication before containment is a common sequencing error.
- Automatic high-impact actions without validation are high-risk distractors.
- Alert quantity is not equal to detection quality.

### Domain 5

- Activity metrics are often substituted for risk outcomes.
- Contractual controls are confused with technical controls.
- Risk acceptance without owner and review date is invalid governance.

## Quick Self-Check Rubric

Before locking an answer, ask:

1. Does this action match the scenario phase and urgency?
2. Is it least-privilege and operationally safe?
3. Can this action be verified and audited?
4. Does this choice reduce risk in measurable terms?
